This RFC proposes a structural definition and classification model for stateless systems based upon the derived invariant “voluntary order without rulers.”

It derives a minimal set of constraints necessary for preserving voluntary coordination among autonomous agents and evaluates coordination mechanisms through invariant-based constraint testing.

This RFC does not prescribe economic systems, governance implementations, or institutional forms; it defines only the structural conditions required for a system to qualify as a stateless society.


## Abstract

RFC-0001 defines a structural classification model for stateless societies. It derives a single invariant — "Voluntary Order Without Rulers" — and from that invariant derives six necessary structural constraints.

Systems satisfying those constraints qualify as stateless under this model; systems violating them do not.

From this invariant it derives six necessary constraints:

1. Agency & Identity
2. No Initiated Coercion
3. Privacy
4. Transparency
5. Exit
6. Interoperability

Any system violating these constraints fails qualification as stateless under this model.


## Introduction

### Purpose

This RFC proposes a structural classification model for stateless systems grounded in a single invariant:

Voluntary Order Without Rulers

The purpose of this document is not to advocate a particular economic system, cultural identity, institutional arrangement, or political faction.

Its purpose is narrower:

To determine the minimal structural conditions required for a system to qualify as a stateless society.

This RFC attempts to operationalize anarchism as a structural model rather than a rhetorical label.

The focus is therefore on classification:
what qualifies, what fails qualification, and why.

The invariant is treated as a constraint to be tested against social mechanisms, institutional forms, and coordination structures.

### Scope

This RFC defines a classification framework.
It does not prescribe implementation.

Specifically, this RFC does not:

- define property norms
- prescribe economic systems
- define governance protocols
- define justice systems
- prescribe enforcement architectures
- resolve historical transition problems
- mandate specific institutional forms

Those are implementation-level concerns belonging to individual spoke systems.

The purpose of this RFC is only to define the structural constraints those systems must satisfy in order to qualify as stateless under the invariant.

This document therefore distinguishes between:

- the hub: the invariant and its constraints
- the spokes: implementation systems built within those constraints.

The hub defines the boundary.
The spokes define possible implementations.

### Methodology
The methodology used throughout this RFC is structural derivation through invariant testing.

The process is consistent:

- Define a structural element.
- Apply the invariant.
- Stress-test the element under edge conditions.
- Identify failure modes.
- Evaluate whether the element preserves or violates voluntary order without rulers.

Results are evaluated through invariant compatibility:

- PASS → the structure preserves the invariant.
- FAIL → the structure introduces rulership, coercive authority, or invariant violation.
- CONDITIONAL → the structure passes or fails depending on whether a stated antecedent holds; the antecedent identifies the invariant-relevant condition, and its resolution may depend on spoke-level inputs (e.g., resource status, disclosed scope).

The analysis is intentionally structural rather than moral.

This RFC does not attempt to determine whether systems are desirable, fair, efficient, virtuous, or culturally preferable.

It attempts only to determine whether they remain structurally compatible with stateless voluntary order.

This RFC was developed through one year of open adversarial review before a public audience of approximately 80,000 people, including extended critique and debate within anarchist and political discourse communities. The current document represents a consolidated refinement of those discussions. As a living document hosted on GitHub, this RFC invites ongoing critique, proposal, and refinement through the issue and PR system.

Academic citations are omitted by design.

The model is presented as a self-contained structural derivation whose claims are intended to stand or fall on their definitions, logical consistency, invariant compatibility, and ability to survive adversarial testing.

## 1. Deriving the Invariant

When defining a stateless system structurally, the first question is:
what qualifies as one?

Not the common meaning, not emotional ideologies, not economic systems, not political preference, but the structural essence of what a stateless system is.

That is where this RFC begins.
The derivation begins from structural primitives.

The purpose of this section is to derive the structural base — the invariant — required for any system to qualify as stateless.

The method is simple:

- Minimal premises.
- Follow logical necessity.

No normative claims. No ideological references. No moral assertions.

Structure.
Nothing more.


### Key Terms

The following definitions serve as the structural base of the derivation.

- **Stateless System** — A coordination system in which no agent or rule-structure possesses structural authority over others. Structural authority refers to the capacity, embedded in the rules of the system, to impose binding outcomes on an agent without that agent's agreement. "Unilateral" in this context does not refer to the number of actors exercising authority. It refers to the absence of the bound party's agreement. A rule binding dissenters is unilateral with respect to those dissenters, regardless of whether one person, many persons, or a rotating majority invokes it.
- **Agent** — An entity capable of intentional action, entering or refusing agreement, and sustaining accountable continuity over time. Agency in this context refers only to structural participation in coordination — not biological, metaphysical, or moral status.
- **Autonomy** — An agent's capacity to act without external imposition of binding obligation.
- **Ruler** — Any agent or rule-structure possessing structural authority to impose binding outcomes on others without their agreement.
- **Order** — Persistent coordination among agents over time.

### The Multi-Agent Condition

At least two agents exist.

Agents interact.

Interaction creates the possibility of conflict.

Persistent coordination among agents requires mechanisms for resolving conflict. No system of order can avoid this condition. Any theory of stateless coordination must therefore answer the structural problem of conflict resolution among autonomous agents.


### Resolution Mechanisms
Any conflict resolution mechanism must answer a single structural question:

Can one agent or rule-structure impose a binding outcome on another who does not agree?

All mechanisms reduce to one of two structural forms.


**A. Binding Without Agreement**

One party, or rule-structure, imposes a binding outcome on another who does not agree. The bound party is obligated regardless of dissent.


**B. Binding With Agreement**

A binding outcome arises only when affected parties participate in its formation. Participation is voluntary. Refusal results in non-participation, not obligation.

Clarification:

If a dissenting party is bound against their will, binding without agreement has occurred.

If a party retains the ability to refuse participation without being bound, binding with agreement has occurred.

Any binding outcome either obligates a dissenting party or it does not.
If it does, binding without agreement has occurred.
If it does not, binding depends on agreement.

There is no intermediate state: either dissenters are bound or they are not.

There is no third structural category.

This distinction precedes any moral framing.


### Structural Consequences of Binding Without Agreement

If binding without agreement is permitted:

- Authority asymmetry emerges.
- A binding hierarchy is created.
- The bound party no longer operates autonomously.

Structural authority resides in the rule permitting such binding, not in the identity of the actor applying it. Rotation of office, majority vote, or temporary delegation does not eliminate authority; it merely redistributes access to it.

A rule that binds dissenters without their agreement constitutes structural authority, even if no single agent permanently holds that authority.

Structural authority is itself a form of strategic advantage.

Strategic advantage creates asymmetric capability; those who possess access to the binding mechanism face structural pressure to use it or be displaced by others who will.

Restraint is not structurally enforced — it depends on discretion, which is itself an expression of authority.

Structural authority creates the capability and incentive for its own expansion.
Power concentration becomes self-reinforcing.

Therefore:
Binding without agreement produces rulers.

A ruler is not defined by title, scale, duration, or permanence — but by structural authority embedded in the rule-system to impose binding outcomes without agreement.


### Incompatibility

If a system permits binding without agreement:

It contains structural authority.

Structural authority constitutes rulership.

It is not stateless.

If binding occurs without agreement:
Participation is not autonomous.

Persistent autonomous coordination and binding without agreement are structurally incompatible.

A system cannot simultaneously preserve autonomy and permit structural authority over dissent.

This remains true even in systems with rotating decision-makers or majority rule. If dissenters are bound without their agreement, the rule binding them functions as authority independent of their consent. The authority resides in the rule-form itself.


### Stability Requirement

A coordination system must remain stable under recursion.

Stability in this context does not mean mere persistence. Hierarchical systems can persist.

Stability here refers specifically to the persistence of a stateless condition under repeated interaction.

If structural authority to bind without agreement exists:

- The binding rule can be invoked repeatedly.
- Repeated invocation produces persistent override capability.
- Persistent override capability constitutes durable structural authority.

A stateless system permitting binding without agreement contains a mechanism capable of negating its own stateless classification.

Structural validity requires that classification remain invariant under iteration.

If binding without agreement is allowed, classification does not remain invariant.

Stability therefore structurally requires prohibition of binding without agreement.

This recursion requirement applies to all later implementation-facing mechanisms. Its application to polycentric enforcement is explicitly tested under Constraint 1: Enforcement Under Recursion.


### Stress Testing the Structure

The derived structure must remain internally consistent under edge cases.

**Self-Defense**

Halting a prior imposed binding does not create new structural authority.
It removes an existing override.
Responsive force aimed at restoring autonomy does not constitute new binding without agreement.
It is not new binding because the prior binding already violated autonomy; responsive force merely removes that violation.

**Delegation**

Authority may be delegated through agreement.
Delegation must remain scope-bound and revocable.
If delegation becomes irrevocable, it becomes structural authority.
Irrevocable delegation recreates rulership.

**Emergency Authority**

Temporary structural authority remains authority regardless of duration.
Time does not alter structure.
If binding occurs without agreement, structural authority exists — whether exercised briefly, cyclically, or permanently.

**Contract Enforcement**

Enforcement consistent with prior agreement does not create new binding without agreement.
Enforcement beyond prior scope does.
Scope defines obligation.
Expansion beyond scope constitutes new binding.


### Collective Agency

Groups, institutions, and associations are coordination structures composed of agents.

They may organize action, establish procedures, pool resources, delegate authority, or authorize agents to act on their behalf. But the underlying acts remain attributable to individual agents.

Membership in a collective does not dissolve individual agency.

A collective therefore possesses no structural claim to agency superior to that of the individuals composing it.

Collective coordination derives its legitimacy from individual agency; individual agency does not derive its legitimacy from the collective.

Delegated authority exists only through the agreement of participating agents and remains constrained by its defined scope.

A collective cannot acquire authority over dissenting agents merely by asserting a collective identity, majority preference, institutional continuity, or common purpose.

If it does, the collective has ceased functioning merely as a coordination structure and has become a ruler.


### Collective Decision-Making

Collective procedures do not alter structure.

If dissenters are bound without agreement, structural authority exists.
If participation is voluntary and exit remains available, autonomy remains intact.

The number of decision-makers does not change the structural condition.
Only agreement alters structure.

The structure remains internally consistent.


### Emergence of the Invariant

To preserve autonomous coordination among multiple agents:

- No rule-structure may embed the authority to bind agents without their agreement.
- Binding must arise from agreement, not imposition.

Agreement thus emerges as the primitive mechanism preserving autonomy.

This prohibition is structurally necessary for stateless qualification.

Therefore, the invariant emerges: Voluntary Order Without Rulers.


### Identification

The structural invariant derived above is commonly referred to as:
Anarchism.

The term follows the structure — not the reverse.

### Classification Claim
This result is not a moral preference.
It is a necessary condition for any system qualifying as stateless.

If structural authority to bind without agreement exists, the system fails qualification.

If autonomous coordination persists without rulers, the system satisfies the invariant.

If structural authority to bind without agreement is permitted in any managed, temporary, rotating, collective, or conditional form, the system contains a ruler at the moment of its exercise.

A structure cannot both contain rulership and qualify as stateless.

Binding without agreement violates invariance.

Its prohibition is therefore necessary for a system to qualify as stateless.


### What This Derivation Does Not Do

This derivation establishes only the minimal invariant required for stateless qualification.

It does not:

- Define property norms.
- Prescribe economic systems.
- Specify governance mechanisms.
- Resolve historical injustice.

Those are implementation-level problems.

Each implementation of stateless society must satisfy the invariant derived here in order to qualify as stateless.



## 2. The Implications of the Invariant

The previous section derived the invariant required for any system to qualify as stateless:
Voluntary Order Without Rulers.

This invariant establishes the structural boundary of a stateless society.

If agents may be bound without their agreement, structural authority emerges and the system ceases to be stateless.
If agents interact through voluntary agreement without rulers, the system satisfies the invariant.

The invariant therefore functions as the defining constraint of a stateless society.

However, typical discourse on stateless societies treats anarchism as a collection of competing ideologies: anarcho-communism, anarcho-capitalism, and numerous other variants. These systems are often presented as mutually exclusive definitions of anarchism.

From a structural perspective this framing is incorrect.

The invariant derived previously is not itself an economic system, cultural program, or institutional design. It defines only the boundary condition under which stateless coordination remains possible.

Within that boundary, many different implementations may exist.

This section therefore introduces a hub-and-spoke model:

- The invariant functions as the hub.
- Implementation systems function as spokes.

Each spoke proposes a way to organize production, property, or social coordination.
However, all spokes remain constrained by the same invariant.

If a spoke introduces binding without agreement, the invariant is violated and the system ceases to qualify as stateless.

The purpose of this section is to clarify this structural relationship.


### Test Case: Anarcho-Communism

Anarcho-communism proposes collective control of production and distribution through systems of shared ownership and mutual aid.

Within this model, economic coordination is organized around communal norms governing production, allocation, and participation.

These norms presume widespread adherence among participants.

If participation in these norms remains voluntary and revocable, the invariant remains satisfied.

However, maintaining shared production systems across a divergent population requires mechanisms capable of responding to non-participation or disagreement.

If such mechanisms impose binding outcomes on individuals who do not agree to those norms, structural authority emerges.

Structural authority violates the invariant.

The failure point is therefore not the normative goal of the system, but the structural problem of enforcing communal norms across agents with differing preferences.

When enforcement mechanisms capable of binding dissenters arise, coordination authority concentrates among those administering the system.

At that point hierarchy reappears and the system ceases to qualify as stateless.

This condition represents constraint drift.

Anarcho-communism therefore represents one possible spoke within the anarchist model.

Like any implementation, it remains compatible with anarchism only while the invariant is preserved.



### Test Case: Anarcho-Capitalism

Anarcho-capitalism proposes market institutions as replacements for state governance.

Agents voluntarily enter contracts, firms, and corporate structures in order to coordinate economic activity.

Within these structures, governance authority is typically assigned through ownership and shareholder mechanisms.

As long as participation in these arrangements remains voluntary and revocable, the invariant remains satisfied.

However, if enforcement mechanisms associated with these institutions acquire the ability to impose binding outcomes on individuals who did not consent to those structures, structural authority emerges.

Structural authority violates the invariant.

At that point the system ceases to qualify as stateless, regardless of whether the authority is exercised by public institutions or private ones.

The concentration of economic power can create structural incentives for such authority to emerge.

When enforcement extends beyond the scope of voluntary agreement, power reorganizes around the mechanisms capable of imposing binding outcomes.

This condition represents constraint drift.


### Constraint Drift

Both examples illustrate the same structural risk: the emergence of mechanisms capable of binding dissenting agents.

Any implementation that permits binding without agreement violates the invariant and ceases to qualify as stateless.

Constraint drift occurs when the shared constraint that defines a system stops constraining behavior.

In the context of this model, that constraint is the invariant: voluntary order without rulers.

When the invariant stops functioning as binding, disputes stop being resolved through principle and begin being resolved through power.
When no shared constraint governs disagreement, conflict does not disappear — it simply changes how it is resolved.

Some systems attempt to manage disagreement through voting procedures rather than agreement.

If dissenting participants remain bound by outcomes they did not agree to, binding without agreement occurs.

Structural authority therefore emerges regardless of how the decision mechanism is organized.
The result is predictable.

The result is structural instability:
division, coercion, and factional competition over the binding mechanism.

The system therefore fails to maintain stable coordination.

What I’m pointing out is that when a system loses its shared constraint on power, long-term stability becomes fragile.

Disputes stop being resolved through principle and begin being resolved through power.

The symptoms are easy to spot:

- No shared constraint.
- Disputes resolved by power.
- Power concentrates.
- Hierarchy follows.

We see this pattern constantly.

The common failure isn’t economic design — it’s what happens when the invariant stops acting as a constraint on power.

Any implementation that permits binding without agreement violates the invariant and ceases to qualify as stateless.


### Structural Consequence

When the invariant stops functioning as a shared constraint, implementation systems drift toward hierarchy.

This failure mode is not specific to any particular ideology.

Different economic systems organize production differently, but they remain subject to the same structural pressure.

If disputes are resolved through power rather than agreement:

- No shared constraint exists.
- Disputes are resolved by power.
- Power concentrates.
- Hierarchy follows.

This pattern reflects constraint drift.

Constraint drift therefore represents the primary structural failure mode of stateless systems.

Any implementation must continually preserve the invariant in order to remain stateless.


### Minimal Constraint

The invariant derived in the previous section establishes the structural boundary of stateless systems.

If binding without agreement is permitted, rulers emerge.

Therefore the prohibition of rulership constitutes the minimal constraint required for stateless coordination.

This constraint is not derived from ideology or preference.

It follows from the structural requirements of autonomous coordination among agents.



## 3. Constraint 1: Agency & Identity

Before we can talk about coercion, privacy, transparency, exit or interoperability, we have to answer something more fundamental:


### What Is an Agent?

If a stateless society's core requirement is “voluntary order without rulers,” then voluntary order requires agents capable of choice.

If agency collapses, the model collapses.

So let’s test it.

An agent in a voluntary system must possess:

1. Intentionality
2. Continuity
3. Attributability


### Intentionality

The ability to form preferences and choose.
Value is what an agent treats as worth choosing.

Without intentionality, consent collapses.


### Continuity
Persistence over time.

Without continuity, obligations collapse.

Continuity may be preserved through:

- Self-attestation (I claim continuity)
- Peer attestation (others verify continuity)
- Cryptographic continuity (signature chains)
- Reputation graphs (network-confirmed history)



### Attributability

Actions and commitments can be linked back to the same locus of agency.

Without attributability, contracts collapse.

Remove any one of these and voluntary order breaks.

Identity cannot be purely performative or transient. It cannot be a label. It cannot be a registry entry.

An agent requires intentionality, continuity and attributability.


### Identity

Identity is the persistent locus of agency that preserves these across time and contexts.
That’s it.

- Not a passport.
- Not a biometric record.
- Not a government-issued number.

Identity exists wherever agency persists and others can attribute commitments to it.

If it requires a ruler to validate it, it fails.

If it dissolves accountability, it fails.


### How Is Agency Continuous Over Time?

What preserves continuity?

- Memory?
- Biology?
- Cryptography?
- Reputation?
- Social recognition?

The invariant does not prescribe the mechanism.

It requires the outcome:
Identity persists insofar as others can reliably attribute past commitments to the same locus of agency.

Continuity is relational.

It does not require:

- A central registry.
- A biological definition.
- A state authority.

It requires recognizability within voluntary networks.

That recognizability may be preserved through:

- Reputation systems
- Cryptographic signatures
- Witness attestation
- Web-of-trust models
- Contractual relationships
- Community recognition

The hub does not mandate the spoke.

It mandates continuity sufficient for accountability.


### Can Identity Be Voluntary?

The test is direct: can an agent discard identity and escape obligations?

- You can discard a name.
- You cannot discard continuity.
- You cannot erase obligations from those who remember.

Obligations attach to agency — not to labels.

Changing:

- A username
- A cryptographic key
- A geographic location
- A community

does not discharge obligations if the underlying agency remains continuous.

If identity reset dissolves obligation, accountability collapses.


Test: Can an agent escape contractual or scope-bound obligation by discarding identity?
**Result:** FAIL

Identity reset as obligation evasion violates attributability and constitutes a form of deception that will be addressed under coercion.



### The Knife Edge

Identity systems fail in two predictable ways.

### Failure Mode A: Pure Anonymity
Test: Can agents act without any traceable continuity?
**Result:** FAIL

If actions cannot be attributed:

- Contracts cannot be enforced.
- Reputation cannot accumulate.
- Trust fragments.

Total anonymity collapses accountability.


### Failure Mode B: Mandatory Identity Infrastructure
Test: Does identity require central issuance, biometric capture, or universal registration?
**Result:** FAIL

If the registry becomes a structural authority over recognition, the issuer becomes a ruler.

That violates the invariant of "voluntary order without rulers."


### Attribution Without Surveillance
There is a distinction that must be maintained.

- Surveillance is continuous, non-consensual observation.
- Attribution is discrete, consent-based verification of commitments.

A stateless system requires attribution. It does not require surveillance.

Test: Does attribution require continuous monitoring of agents?
**Result:** FAIL

Attribution only requires that past commitments remain linkable to present agency.


Permitted:

- Voluntary reputation records
- Cryptographic verification
- Witness testimony
- Mutual attestation


Prohibited:

- Compulsory tracking
- Forced biometric enrollment
- Continuous monitoring without consent

Attribution preserves accountability. Surveillance creates rulership.

### Voluntary Recognition
Identity is not granted.
It is recognized.

Recognition is an act of other agents.

An agent is recognized when others:

- Accept their commitments as binding.
- Hold them accountable for past actions.
- Treat them as continuous over time.

Recognition is polycentric.

No single recognition binds all others. No registry defines existence.

Recognition does not grant permission to be. It enables accountability.

### Test Cases

The following tests apply the invariant to concrete identity models:

State-Issued Mandatory ID
Test: Does mandatory central registration preserve accountability without creating a ruler?
**Result:** FAIL

Pure Unlinkable Pseudonymity
Test: Does complete unlinkability preserve accountability?
**Result:** FAIL

Reputation-Linked Pseudonym
Test: Does a persistent pseudonym with linked reputation preserve accountability without coercion?
**Result:** PASS

Voluntary Web-of-Trust
Test: Does community-attested identity preserve accountability without centralization?
**Result:** PASS

Identity Reset on Exit
Test: Can an agent discard obligations by discarding identity?
**Result:** FAIL

Identity reset is impossible in principle because continuity is inherent to agency. One may change names, locations, or keys, but the underlying locus of agency persists. Obligations attach to that continuity, not to its current label.

Biometric Mandate
Test: Does compulsory biometric capture preserve voluntary order?
**Result:** FAIL

The invariant holds.


### Special Cases

Full agency is not present in all humans at all times. Children, temporary incapacity, and permanent cognitive disability require spoke-level frameworks for representation and fiduciary responsibility.

These frameworks must themselves respect this constraint and those that follow.

False attribution — claiming to be another agent — violates continuity and attributability. Remedies for false attribution will be addressed as coercion and dispute resolution constraints of spoke implementations.


### Enforcement Assumption

Constraint 1 assumes accountability is enforced polycentrically.

Through:

- Reputation
- Arbitration
- Insurance
- Bonding
- Exclusion
- Escrow

The hub does not prescribe the enforcement mechanism. It only requires that identity remain compatible with enforcement without creating a ruler.


### Enforcement Under Recursion

Polycentric enforcement must remain compatible with the Stability Requirement.

Test: Through repeated voluntary interaction, one arbitration, protection, or enforcement network becomes dominant.

**Result:** PASS

Dominance resulting from voluntary participation does not create rulership. Market share, scale, or network effects alone do not constitute structural authority.

Test: The dominant network prevents competing enforcement systems from operating, asserts jurisdiction over nonparticipants, prohibits agents from choosing alternative mechanisms, or binds agents outside delegated scope.

**Result:** FAIL

At that point dominance has become jurisdictional monopoly. The network possesses structural authority over agents who did not delegate it and therefore functions as a ruler.

A stateless enforcement system may become dominant through voluntary selection. It may not convert dominance into authority over nonparticipants or competitors.

The Stability Requirement therefore applies recursively to enforcement itself: enforcement mechanisms must remain subject to agency, scope, exit, and interoperability under repeated interaction.

The hub classifies the point at which enforcement drift violates the invariant; it does not prescribe mechanisms guaranteeing that such drift cannot occur. Preventing or correcting that drift remains a spoke-level design burden. Exit and interoperability provide structural pressures against consolidation, but do not guarantee continued compliance.

### Observed Result

Partial anonymity is compatible with voluntary order. Unaccountable anonymity is not.

Identity must therefore be:

- Continuous enough for accountability.
- Voluntarily recognized.
- Free from compulsory infrastructure.
- Compatible with polycentric enforcement.

Anything that collapses accountability or creates a sovereign identity authority fails.

Constraint 1 establishes the foundation.

- Without agency, there is no voluntary order.
- Without continuity, there is no obligation.
- Without attributability, there is no contract.

Identity is not a registry entry.
It is the minimum structure required for freedom to mean anything at all.


## 4. Constraint 2: No Initiated Coercion

Coercion is the initiated override of an individual’s choice.

And coercion takes many forms:

- Physical Coercion
- Institutional Coercion
- Economic Coercion
- Information Coercion
- Threat of Force
- Social Coercion
- Exclusion
- Access, Leverage, and Scope
- Resource Seizure (Theft)

So let's take the invariant "voluntary order without rulers" and apply it to human conflict.

To test it, we must isolate an atomic property of all conflict: initiation.

Initiation, in this model, refers to the first act that violates the voluntary order, specifically through:
- (1) force or threat of force,
- (2) fraud/deception that induces non-voluntary choice, or
- (3) the use of a voluntary agreement to force a choice.

**"Force a choice"** in this context means imposing a new obligation, penalty, or restriction not present in the original consent scope.

**"Scope"** in this context refers to terms that are explicit, finite, determinate, bounded, and knowable at the time of choice.

**"Excess"** means imposing any new obligation, penalty, or restriction not contained in that scope.

A clause that allows unilateral, unlimited future obligation expansion constitutes implicit rulership and fails the invariant.

Any initiated override not responsive to a prior violation establishes rulership.

"without rulers" is a core evaluation — so the invariant has to reject it.

### Parameterized Resource Status

Constraint 2 does not determine which resources fall within an agent's legitimate agency. Property, stewardship, possession, allocation, and related resource norms are implementation-level questions belonging to spokes.

Initial-acquisition rules are defined by implementing spokes; the hub requires only that their operation not depend upon initiated override of another agent or structural authority over dissenters.

The coercion test accepts resource status as an input, provided that status was itself established through mechanisms compatible with the invariant.

Once a resource is validly under an agent's agency, its non-consensual appropriation constitutes an override of that agent's choice and fails the invariant.

Where resource status itself is disputed, the hub does not resolve the underlying property theory. That determination belongs to the applicable voluntary spoke or consent-scoped agreement.

### Coercion is an Action

Coercion, by definition, is an initiated action intended to override an individual's choice.

Through this understanding the evaluation becomes simple:

Does coercing someone violate the invariant of "voluntary order without rulers"?
Yes.

- An initiated override of choice introduces a ruler.
- Rulers in all forms violate the invariant.
- Therefore initiated coercion fails the invariant.

But what about Coercion as a Reaction?

### Coercion as a Reaction

Let's look at this from the perspective of self-defense.
It's by far the most common use case.

Test: Physical coercion is initiated by Party A.
**Result:** FAIL
This is a direct action taken to influence Party B into an involuntary action.

Test: Party B responds to the initiated coercion with physical coercion.
**Result:** PASS
Responsive force halts the violation of the invariant rather than creating one.

Since Party B does not initiate the override but acts to stop it, Party B does not introduce rulership.

The evaluation changes when force is responsive rather than initiated. The logical condition being tested is initiation, not force itself.

Party B, in effect, enforces the invariant by not allowing coercion to occur.

This means that self-defense is not coercion, because coercion is defined as initiation.

However, there is a line.

Self-defense isn't a blank check. It can be abused and taken advantage of.
Responsive force must be limited to halting or rectifying the initiated violation — not expanding control beyond it.

Otherwise, the analysis returns to a violation of the invariant.

The remaining forms of coercion can now be tested directly.



### Other Manifestations of Coercion

Case 1:
Threat of Force: "I will hurt you if you don't comply."

Test: An initiated communication designed to override choice through fear. It establishes a conditional rulership.

**Result:** FAIL

It's the blueprint for rulership, even if the hammer hasn't dropped.

Case 2:
Institutional Coercion (e.g., Taxation, Draft)

Test: An organization claims a right to override "voluntary order without rulers" (take property, conscript labor, impose taxation).

**Result:** FAIL

The institution is a literal, formalized ruler.

Case 3:
Economic Coercion

Test: "Accept this low wage or starve."

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

If Party A did not create or maintain the relevant deprivation through initiated coercion: PASS.
If Party A created the compelled condition through initiated force, fraud, or other invariant violation: FAIL.

Scarcity itself is not coercion. Where Party A did not initiate the condition producing Party B's lack of alternatives, Party A is offering terms for voluntary cooperation. Withdrawal of an offer is not aggression.

Questions concerning historical claims predating the model's T=0 boundary remain governed by the Historical vs. Prophylactic Justice clarification below.

### Social Coercion (Ostracism, Shunning):

Test: A Group Threatening Violence

**Result:** FAIL

A group threatening violence fails the invariant test. While a group can be formed under voluntary order, and a group doesn't necessarily have rulers, participating agents who use the group structure to threaten or initiate coercion exercise rulership through the group as a coordination vector.

Test: Refusing to Interact or Do Business With Someone

**Result:** PASS

Exclusion is a foundational mechanism of voluntary association. The ability to refuse interaction is necessary for participation to remain voluntary.


### Deception & Fraud

Deception / Fraud is the intentional action to induce a non-voluntary choice, through lying or intentional concealment of material facts.

**Result:** FAIL

Deception limits or removes the choice of the individual by hiding the truth. Fraud is a form of deception and as such also fails against the invariant.

Subclass: Information Coercion

   Information Coercion refers strictly to material deception or falsification in the context of inducing agreement. Mere persuasion or rhetorical framing does not constitute coercion unless it involves fraud.


### Monopoly without Initiation

Test: What if through purely voluntary exchanges, someone gains control over essential infrastructure?

**Result:** PASS

Monopoly alone doesn't violate the invariant. Only initiated override would.


### Omissions vs Commissions

Does withholding essential resources (water, food, energy) constitute initiation of coercion?


Test: Water company has monopoly over water — charges for service.

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- If scarcity/control was not established through initiated coercion: PASS.
- If it was: FAIL.

Test: Water company has monopoly over water — withdraws service for demands.

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- Withdrawal within existing contract: PASS.
- Withdrawal conditioned on new obligation: FAIL.

When enforcement exceeds the originally consented terms or alters the scope of obligation rulership is being initiated.
Not because water is essential.
Not because monopoly is evil.
But because they are using previously granted access or contract as leverage to extend control over choice.

Simply put, it converts voluntary agreement into a domination mechanism.


### Access, Leverage, and Scope

Scarcity is a universal condition. Time, resources, attention — all are finite.

In a state of scarcity, every interaction involves leverage. I have something you want. You have something I need. We negotiate.

The invariant does not abolish scarcity. It governs how agents interact within it.

Leverage is the passive fact of having something another values.

Coercion is the initiated act of using force or fraud to override choice in order to seize it.

When a worker accepts a low wage, the employer's leverage comes from owning capital and offering voluntary exchange. The worker's leverage comes from their labor and the ability to refuse. Where the employer did not create or maintain the compelled condition through initiated coercion, the “or starve” constraint arises from scarcity rather than from the employer's initiation.

To claim coercion in that case is to claim an entitlement to the other party's resources, which is not permitted by the invariant. Enforcing such an entitlement would itself require initiated coercion.

In summary:

- Scarcity creates leverage.
- Leverage is not coercion.
- Coercion is the initiated violation of choice within the context of scarcity.

This distinction is the firewall between voluntary exchange and systemic theft.



### Resource Seizure (Theft)

Resource Seizure (theft) is the non-consensual appropriation of resources under another’s agency.

What if I take an apple that belongs to someone?

**Result:** FAIL

You have taken a resource that is under another's agency. You may not have used force against them, but you've denied them the choice to give it to you resulting in the override of agency.


### What if a Group Coerces?

The invariant applies to actions. A "group" cannot act; only individuals within a group can.

When a group forms to coerce, it's the members' actions that violate the invariant. The "group" is merely the vector. Responsibility for the action remains attributable to the participating agents.

Any coordinated initiation of coercion constitutes rulership regardless of scale. Rulers of any kind violate the invariant.


### Observed Results of Constraint Testing

The invariant has now been tested against the various forms of coercion including:
- (1) force or threat of force,
- (2) fraud/deception that induces non-voluntary choice, and
- (3) the use of a voluntary agreement to excess beyond its originally consented scope.

Access, Leverage, and Scope have been tested as forms of coercion, and every instance of coercion creates a point of rulership.

What unifies every FAIL result?
Not force. Not harm. Not inequality.

Initiation.

- In every case that failed, someone acted first to override another's choice.
- In every case that passed, no such first move occurred.

This reveals something about the invariant itself:
"Voluntary order without rulers" is not a prohibition on force — it's a prohibition on the *first* use of force, fraud, or scope expansion.

The rule is not "no coercion." The rule is "no initiated coercion."

Because once initiation occurs, the responder is no longer choosing freely — they're reacting. And reaction, even forceful reaction, does not create rulership. It restores the condition under which voluntary order is possible.

This is why the constraint resolves to this single prohibition. All else is implementation.

This premise was not assumed at the outset; it emerged as the logical conclusion. Under this derivation, No Initiated Coercion is the only universal prohibition required by the invariant. Other rules remain implementation-level, provided they do not violate it.

This constraint — No Initiated Coercion — now becomes our second structural constraint in the model.

The next constraints (Privacy, Transparency, Exit) will be derived from the same invariant.

### Clarifications for Implementation (Not Modifications)
To prevent common misinterpretations by future implementers, the following clarifications are noted:

**"Force" vs. "Coercion":** The document correctly distinguishes these. Force is a physical capability; coercion is its initiated application to override choice. This distinction is critical for understanding defensive and rectificatory force.

**The "Truth Primitive":** As noted, Information Coercion is correctly subsumed under fraud/deception. The model implicitly establishes truthful disclosure within the context of a proposed agreement as a prerequisite for voluntary choice. Fraud vitiates consent by violating this primitive.

**The "Scope" Mechanism:** The prohibition on using a voluntary agreement to impose "excess beyond its originally consented scope" is the model's immune response to contractual Trojan horses. This prevents voluntary agreements from metastasizing into instruments of domination. Future Constraints within this RFC covering Transparency and Exit will provide the mechanisms to operationalize this.

**Historical vs. Prophylactic Justice:** The constraint is forward-looking (prophylactic). It establishes the rule from point T=0 forward. It is silent on rectification for historical injustices predating T=0. Implementers in spoke societies must address this transition, but any rectification that itself uses initiated coercion would violate the new invariant.


## 5. Constraint 3: Privacy

Privacy is defined as:
The voluntary control over the disclosure of information concerning oneself and one’s voluntary associations.

Privacy protects that control within legitimate domain boundaries. It does not grant authority over truthful speech generated by another agent within that agent’s domain.

Legitimate domain includes information generated within one’s person and voluntarily directed communications, and excludes independently observed facts and experiences generated by other agents within their own domains.


### Privacy as Absolutes

Case 1: No Privacy

Test: Is all information about an individual accessible without consent?

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- If disclosure is consensual: PASS.
- If disclosure is non-consensual: FAIL.

Case 2: Absolute Privacy

Test: Can an individual prohibit others from speaking truthfully about their own experiences?

**Result:** FAIL

If I can prevent you from speaking truthfully about your own experiences with me, I assert control over your speech and memory.
Absolute privacy would grant an individual veto power over another’s truthful speech, thereby creating unilateral authority inconsistent with voluntary order.

Privacy cannot override another individual's agency.


### Category 1: Personal Privacy vs Voluntary Disclosure

Cases: Healthcare / Disease, Financial Records, Personal Communication, Biometric Capture

Test: Is the information gathered, stored, or disclosed with individual consent?

Principle:
An agent controls information generated within their legitimate domain (body, agency, communications).
Sharing is voluntary.
Non-consensual capture = initiated coercion.
Non-consensual disclosure = initiated coercion.

Case 1:

Health Records

Test: Is medical data disclosed with patient consent?
**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- With consent: PASS.
- Without consent: FAIL.

Biometric Capture (e.g., forced fingerprinting, facial scanning)

Test: Was biometric data taken without consent or contractual scope?
**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- Non-consensual capture: FAIL.
- Voluntary contractual system (e.g., access control you opt into): PASS.

Privacy here protects bodily and informational domain control.



### Category 2: Social & Reputational Privacy
Cases: Reputation Systems, Doxxing / Public Exposure, Membership Lists, Public/Private Acts


Test: Does disclosure serve voluntary social coordination, or does it initiate coercion?

Core Principle:
Reputation is a voluntary social mechanism.
Truthful disclosure of one’s own experience is not coercion.
Coercive exposure requires initiated violation (fraud, trespass, threat).


### Reputation Systems
Test: Are reviews or reputation reports, based on voluntary interactions and truthful experience valid?
**Result:** PASS

Truthful reputation sharing does not override agency.


### Doxxing

Test: Was the information obtained through initiated coercion (fraud, intrusion, threat)?

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- Information obtained through intrusion or coercion: FAIL.
- Truthful information already publicly available or voluntarily disclosed: PASS.

Intent alone does not constitute initiation; the method of acquisition determines violation.
Truthful publication alone does not constitute coercion absent fraud, intrusion, or threat.

### Membership Lists
Test: Was confidentiality contractually agreed upon?

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- Disclosure violating agreed scope: FAIL.
- No confidentiality agreement: PASS.

Scope governs obligation.


### Public Acts
Test: Does an act occur in a publicly observable domain?

**Result:** PASS

Public acts, by definition, generate public knowledge. No privacy claim exists over what others independently observe.
Observation from within one's own domain (public street, one's property, shared commercial space) of acts visible from that domain does not constitute intrusion.

The observer's truthful testimony about their own experience is their speech, not the subject's information.



### Category 3: Commercial & Contractual Privacy
Cases: Email Marketing, Contracts, Corporate Secrecy, Data Aggregation

Test: Is privacy being used to facilitate voluntary exchange, or to commit fraud?

Core Principle:
Contracts may include privacy clauses.
Fraudulent secrecy fails the invariant.

### Email Marketing
Test: Was contact information obtained and used within consented scope?

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- Opt-in marketing: PASS.
- Harvested or deceptive use: FAIL.

### Corporate Secrecy
Test: Is secrecy hiding coercion or fraud?

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- Protecting trade secrets within voluntary contracts: PASS.
- Concealing harmful deception (e.g., known defects): FAIL.

Fraud overrides consent.


### Data Aggregation
Test: Was data collected within agreed scope?

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- Transparent, consent-based aggregation: PASS.
- Hidden or deceptive aggregation: FAIL.

Scope expansion without consent constitutes informational coercion.


### Category 4: Informational Power & Asymmetry

Cases: Surveillance, Whistleblowing, Property Intrusion
Test: Is the act initiated coercion, or responsive exposure of coercion?


### Surveillance
Test: Is observation conducted without consent or via intrusion?

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- Coercive surveillance (e.g., forced monitoring, property intrusion): FAIL.
- Observation of publicly visible acts from one’s own domain: PASS.

Privacy protects against intrusion — not against being seen in public.


### Property Intrusion for Information
Test: Was property entered or accessed without consent?
**Result:** FAIL

Intrusion is initiated coercion.


### Whistleblowing
Test: Does disclosure expose initiated coercion or fraud?

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- Exposure of coercion to halt violation: PASS (responsive).
- Disclosure unrelated to rectification: evaluated under scope.

Whistleblowing functions analogously to self-defense in Constraint 2. It is justified when halting or rectifying initiated coercion.
Disclosure must be proportionate to the coercion being halted and limited to rectification scope.


### Category 5: Special Cases
Cases: Children / Dependents, Guardianship

Test: Who consents, and within what fiduciary scope?

Core Principle:
Guardianship is temporary and fiduciary — not sovereign.
Children lack full agency capacity.
Guardians manage privacy rights on their behalf until agency matures.

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- Fiduciary management within protective scope: PASS.
- Use of guardianship to expand coercive authority: FAIL.

Privacy is not secrecy. It is domain control.

Medical decisions
Guardian consents within scope of child's welfare. Use of child's medical data beyond treatment scope? FAIL.

Social media
The child, upon reaching agency, retains no ongoing obligation to honor disclosures or obligations by their guardians, made during guardianship, though third parties may have independently acquired knowledge through lawful means.


### Insights

Absolute privacy, defined as total prohibition of truthful disclosure by others, is coercive.
Non-consensual capture or disclosure within legitimate domain boundaries is likewise coercive.

Valid consent requires truthful representation of material facts within scope. Fraud constitutes intentional misrepresentation of such facts.
Disputes concerning material fact must be adjudicated through voluntary mechanisms defined by implementing spokes.

Privacy does not create new rights; it constrains how information flows between agents under the invariant.


### Observed Result of Constraint Testing

Applying the invariant to privacy yields a consistent rule:

Non-consensual informational intrusion = initiated coercion.
Contractual confidentiality within scope = legitimate.
Truthful speech within one’s domain = legitimate.
Fraudulent concealment = coercion.
Absolute informational control over others = rulership.

Therefore, consent governs the disclosure and capture of information within legitimate domain boundaries.

Constraint 3 now joins Constraints 1 and 2 as a structural boundary.

Privacy under a stateless society is neither secrecy nor surveillance, but the consent-governed boundary between domains of agency.


## 6. Constraint 4: Transparency

To apply the invariant — “voluntary order without rulers” — to transparency, we must first clarify what transparency is not.

Because most people immediately collapse transparency into one of two authoritarian failure modes: secrecy or surveillance.

Both fail.


### What Transparency Isn't
Transparency is not:

- surveillance
- forced disclosure
- public exposure
- mandatory visibility

These are privacy violations under Constraint 3.
Transparency overriding privacy is not balance.
It’s rulership.

As we previously discovered privacy governs domain.
Transparency governs participation.

Those are not competing principles.
They are dual constraints operating at different layers of the same system.

Privacy protects domain control.
Transparency protects consent integrity.

Neither overrides the other.


### The Structural Question
Apply the invariant.

Can voluntary order function if information relevant to agreement is hidden?
No.

Can voluntary order function if all information about all agents is forcibly public?
No.

Transparency therefore fails in two symmetrical ways:

- Failure Mode A: Opaque Systems
- Failure Mode B: Compulsory Transparency

Both violate the invariant, but for opposite reasons.

Opaque systems destroy informed consent.
Compulsory transparency destroys domain control.
One creates hidden domination.
The other creates surveillance authority.

Both create rulers, thus violating the invariant.


### Transparency Is Not About Existence

This is the distinction most people miss.

Transparency is not about individuals existing in public.
Transparency is about systems of coordination.
Privacy governs personal domain.

Transparency governs decision-impacting information within voluntary participation.
Transparency is triggered by participation — not by existence.
You do not owe transparency because you exist.

You owe transparency when you voluntarily participate in a defined coordination system.
That distinction matters enormously.

Because the moment transparency becomes attached to mere existence:

- surveillance emerges
- social coercion emerges
- informational hierarchy emerges

And voluntary order collapses into rulership.


### Transparency Assumes Agency

This is where the architecture stack starts connecting together.

Transparency assumes:

- attributable agents
- intentional participation
- continuity of obligation
- capacity for informed consent

This is Constraint 1.

Without agency:

- disclosure has no anchor
- consent has no meaning
- fraud becomes trivial
- accountability collapses

If participants cannot reasonably identify:

- who acted
- who disclosed
- who concealed
- who altered terms

then transparency becomes performative theater.

Voluntary coordination requires attributable participation.

Otherwise concealment becomes indistinguishable from manipulation.


### Definition

Transparency is the consent-governed disclosure of material information necessary for voluntary coordination within a defined scope of participation.
It applies to systems of coordination.
Not to existence itself.

Material information is information that a reasonable participant would consider relevant to their decision to enter, remain within or act inside a voluntary coordination system.

Participation within a voluntary coordination system constitutes standing consent to its agreed transparency rules.

But standing consent has limits. Standing consent cannot expand beyond defined scope. Any unilateral expansion constitutes scope violation under Constraint 2.
That distinction matters.

Because “standing consent” is one of the easiest places for systems to quietly become authoritarian.

A platform says:
“You agreed to transparency.”

Then slowly expands:

- disclosure requirements
- data collection
- monitoring
- informational access

far beyond original scope.

That’s not transparency.
That’s coercive scope expansion.

Constraint 2 already classified that failure: binding beyond agreement.


### Transparency Collapses Without Boundaries

Transparency collapses without:

- consent
- materiality
- scope

The moment disclosure exceeds material relevance, transparency becomes surveillance.
The moment participation ceases to be voluntary, transparency becomes coercion.
The moment scope expands without agreement, transparency becomes rulership.

Transparency is not:
“Everyone gets to know everything.”

Transparency is:
“Participants receive the information necessary to preserve informed consent within agreed scope.”

That’s the boundary.

### Failure Mode A: Opaque Systems
Case:
A voluntary coordination system conceals material information from participants.

Examples:

- hidden financial liabilities in a cooperative
- secret bylaw amendments
- concealed contractual obligations
- undisclosed algorithmic manipulation affecting transactions

Apply the test:
Can consent remain valid if material information is concealed?

No. Because concealment materially alters participation decisions.
That means the resulting participation was not fully voluntary.

Fraud vitiates consent. Fraud is initiated coercion under Constraint 2.

Imagine joining a cooperative that secretly carries catastrophic debt.

You contribute:

- labor
- money
- dependency
- long-term planning

Then discover leadership concealed liabilities the entire time. That is not voluntary coordination. It is induced participation under false pretenses — fraud.

If you can’t show the rules, you’re not running a voluntary system.

Opaque systems create hidden domination because participants cannot meaningfully evaluate risk, obligation, exposure, dependency without access to material information. And without informed consent voluntary order collapses.

The opposite failure is just as destructive.


### Failure Mode B: Compulsory Transparency
Case:
Participants are compelled to disclose:

- full personal history
- unrelated communications
- private associations
- unrelated behavioral data

in order to participate.

Apply the test:

Does compulsory disclosure preserve voluntary order?

No. Because forced disclosure violates domain control under the Privacy constraint.
Systems that make this error drift toward informational authoritarianism without intending to.

They think:
“Wanting access to information creates entitlement to it.”

It doesn’t.

Transparency does not mean:
“You get to know everything about everyone.”

It means “Participants receive material information relevant to informed consent within agreed scope.” That’s it.

The moment disclosure exceeds scope:

- surveillance emerges
- chilling effects emerge
- centralized informational power emerges
- coercive hierarchy emerges

Transparency cannot override privacy.
Ever.


### The Transparency Boundary

Transparency is required when:

- participation is voluntary
- information materially affects consent
- disclosure falls within agreed scope

Transparency is prohibited when:

- disclosure exceeds scope
- disclosure is compelled absent participation
- disclosure becomes surveillance

Transparency must remain:

- limited
- material
- consent-based
- scope-bound

The filter is binary.
Either disclosure preserves informed voluntary participation or it doesn’t.

### Test Cases

Case 1: Organizational Transparency

Financial reporting within a cooperative:

- Disclosed liabilities within agreed governance scope: PASS
- Hidden insolvency risk: FAIL
- Forced public disclosure of unrelated member data: FAIL

Case 2: Governance Transparency

Rule changes:

- Advance notice within disclosed amendment procedures: PASS
- Retroactive undisclosed rule changes: FAIL

You cannot claim participation is voluntary while secretly changing the conditions of participation.
That’s governance through concealment.

Case 3: Algorithmic Transparency

Platform coordination systems:

- Disclosed ranking logic materially affecting transactions: PASS
- Hidden algorithmic suppression affecting outcomes: FAIL

If a platform secretly manipulates visibility, pricing, or reach while presenting itself as neutral — participants are no longer consenting to actual conditions.
They are consenting to a fiction.

Case 4: Personal Transparency

- Mandatory disclosure of unrelated personal data: FAIL
- Voluntary disclosure within contractual scope: PASS

Participation does not erase domain control.

The privacy constraint still applies in every participation context.

### The Unifying Principle
Transparency protects consent integrity.
Privacy protects domain control.
Exit protects consent revocability.

Together, these constraints prevent voluntary systems from calcifying into:

- hidden hierarchy
- surveillance authority
- informational coercion
- permanent asymmetry

This is the architecture stack defined across this RFC series.
These constraints are not competing values.
They are structural boundaries.

And each one exists to preserve the invariant: voluntary order without rulers.

### Observed Result

Without transparency: voluntary order degrades into hidden domination.

With absolute transparency: voluntary order degrades into surveillance authority.

Only consent-bound transparency survives the invariant test.
Transparency preserves consent integrity.
Privacy preserves domain control.
Exit preserves consent revocability (addressed in the next section).

Together, they prevent voluntary systems from collapsing into hidden hierarchy.

## 7. Constraint 5: Exit

If we apply the invariant — “voluntary order without rulers” — to obligations that were consented to, then the opposite must also be true: consent can be revoked.
If consent cannot be revoked, it was never voluntary.
At the same time, if revocation dissolves all obligation, consent becomes meaningless.

Exit conditions may serve as enforcement mechanisms, provided they do not violate the coercion constraint.

The central question becomes:
Can consent ever be considered valid if revocation would cause catastrophic harm to the other party?

This constraint addresses that question.


### Structural Role
Transparency ensures consent is informed.
Exit ensures consent remains revocable.

Without transparency, consent is uninformed.
Without exit, consent becomes permanent subordination.
Without scope-bound obligation, consent becomes meaningless.

Exit is the revocation primitive of voluntary order — the withdrawal of consent to future participation in a voluntary coordination system, consistent with previously agreed scope.

Exit governs future participation.
It does not nullify completed exchanges or accrued obligations.

Exit does not erase history.
It governs continuation.

Exit operates within a field of related concepts, each governed by prior constraints and necessary for preserving voluntary order.

- **Consent Revocation** — The withdrawal of future participation in a coordination system.
- **Obligation** — A commitment within scope that survives until fulfilled or discharged.
- **Scope** — Explicit, finite, determinate, bounded, and knowable at the time of consent.
- **Dependency** — A condition of reliance created voluntarily within scope.
- **Externalities** — Effects on third parties not party to the agreement.
- **Catastrophic Harm** — Severe harm arising from exit that exceeds obligations defined within scope at the time of consent.

Disputes concerning scope or catastrophic harm are resolved through voluntary mechanisms defined by implementing spokes.

### Failure Mode A — No Exit

Irrevocable participation → permanent subordination → FAIL.

Irrevocable participation creates permanent subordination.
Permanent subordination is rulership under another name.

Voluntary order requires the possibility of withdrawal.

### Failure Mode B — Absolute Exit

Revocation nullifies obligation → contracts collapse → FAIL.

Revocation that nullifies accrued obligation dissolves contract and continuity.
Without enforceable obligation, continuity and accountability collapse.

Voluntary order requires enforceable scope.

### Dependency

Dependency created within disclosed scope is governed by that scope.

If two parties agree to a five-year lease, foreseeable dependency within that period is part of the bargain.

If a supplier builds capacity based on a month-to-month agreement, that dependency is reckless; no obligation survives termination.

Dependency created through concealment is a Transparency failure.

Reckless dependency without agreement creates no new obligation.

Voluntary order does not guarantee protection from poor planning.
It guarantees protection from undisclosed domination.

### Externalities

Third-party reliance absent agreement does not create obligation.

If Alice and Bob contract, and Carol builds her affairs around their continued relationship without agreement, Carol’s reliance does not bind Alice or Bob.

Otherwise voluntary order collapses into universal liability.

Obligation arises from consent, not proximity.

### Catastrophic Harm

Catastrophic harm does not invalidate exit.

It constrains execution.

If catastrophic harm is foreseeable within scope, remedies must be defined within scope — notice periods, restitution formulas, escrow, bonding.

If catastrophic harm arises from hidden dependency beyond disclosed scope, the failure lies in Transparency.

If catastrophic harm can be prevented only by forced continuation absent prior consent, such continuation constitutes initiated coercion.

Exit must remain possible.
Remedies must remain scope-bound.

Harm constrains execution, not permissibility.


### Hard Case Test: Critical Infrastructure

Case: A surgeon attempts to exit mid-operation.
Case: A nuclear plant operator exits mid-shift.
Case: An air traffic controller abandons post.

Test: Does exit invalidate obligation where catastrophic harm is imminent?

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- If the obligation to complete the shift or procedure was within disclosed scope, exit is constrained by that scope (notice periods, bonding, duty cycles).
- If no such obligation was defined, forced continuation constitutes initiated coercion.

Conclusion:

Catastrophic harm does not nullify exit.
It reinforces the necessity of scope-defined duty.

### Consent Across Time

A recurring objection to the invariant takes the following form:

an agent consents once to a rule-system, and that single act of consent is claimed to legitimize all future bindings the system produces — including bindings the agent later dissents from.

Constitutions, social contracts, and perpetual memberships are common examples.

Apply the test:
Does consent at time T₀ authorize binding a dissenting agent at time T₁?

Only within the scope consented to at T₀.

Time does not alter structure. Consent is an act with a defined scope; it is not a standing transfer of authority. An agreement either covers the later obligation — explicitly, finitely, and knowably — or it does not.

If it does, enforcement at T₁ is enforcement of prior agreement and passes under Constraint 2.

If it does not, the later binding is binding without agreement, regardless of how much time has passed or how the original consent was framed.

Consent at T₀ cannot be stretched to cover outcomes the agent could not have known, terms that changed after consent, or obligations that exceed the original scope.

When a system treats an initial act of consent as a blanket authorization for all future rules it may produce, the consent has ceased to function as agreement and begun to function as allegiance. Allegiance enforced against dissent is rulership.

Test: A constitution ratified by one generation binds the dissenting members of the next.
**Result:** FAIL

The ratifiers consented for themselves. Their descendants never agreed, and no mechanism of exit or scoped obligation was offered. Binding them is binding without agreement.

Test: An agent joins a coordination system whose rules are fixed, disclosed, and scope-bound at the time of joining, and later dissents from an enforcement action taken within that disclosed scope.
**Result:** PASS

The enforcement executes what was actually agreed to. Dissent at the moment of enforcement does not retroactively void prior consent within scope.

Test: A system reserves the right to change its rules after joining, and applies a new rule to a participant who did not agree to it, citing their original act of joining.
**Result:** FAIL

The original consent did not cover the new rule. Scope expansion without renewed agreement is initiated coercion under Constraint 2.

The distinction is not when consent was given, but what it covered. Consent persists exactly as far as its scope — and no further. Systems that require ongoing legitimacy must therefore fix their scope permanently, obtain renewed consent for changes, or preserve exit. Any fourth option reintroduces rulers through the calendar.

### Relationship to Coercion

Withdrawal of participation, even if harmful, is not coercion unless:

- It breaches scope, or
- It is accompanied by initiated force or fraud.

Leverage is not coercion.

The choice not to continue cooperation does not create rulership.

### Enforcement Compatible with the Invariant

Exit enforcement must avoid initiated coercion.

Permitted mechanisms include:

- Escrow
- Bonds
- Notice periods
- Arbitration
- Reputation systems
- Exclusion

Not permitted:

- Physical retention
- Forced continuation beyond scope
- Coercive prevention of withdrawal

Forceful retention is justified only when responsive to fraud or initiated coercion already within scope (see Constraint 2: Coercion as a Reaction).

### Observed Result

What unifies every FAIL result?

Not the presence of exit.
Not the absence of exit.
But the severing of obligation from consent.

Where consent cannot be revoked, hierarchy emerges.
Where obligation cannot survive revocation, continuity dissolves.

Voluntary order requires both:

- Revocable consent.
- Enforceable scope.

Only their coexistence satisfies the invariant.

Exit, together with Transparency, prevents hierarchy from calcifying.

Exit is not a feature of voluntary order. It is its safeguard.



## 8. Constraint 6: Interoperability

Most anarchist discussions assume their spoke will ultimately scale, absorb, or out-compete the others.

Voluntary out-competition does not violate the invariant. Universalization through imposed authority does.

A stateless society cannot depend upon any spoke becoming universally sovereign.

Therefore the question is not "Which system wins?"

The question is "How can multiple voluntary systems coexist and exchange without creating rulership?"

If interoperability requires domination, it fails.
If isolation is the only alternative, the model fragments into tribal enclaves.

### The Structural Question
Can two systems with different internal rules interact without one ruling the other?

If not, statelessness reduces to factionalism.
If yes, interoperability must be grounded somewhere deeper than any spoke.

The invariant points to the only possible foundation: agency itself.
The grounding is the individual agent.

Spokes do not interoperate. Agents do.


### Definition
Interoperability is the consent-governed exchange between agents operating under different voluntary systems, without either system asserting authority beyond explicitly agreed scope.

It requires:

- Recognizable agency (Constraint 1)
- No initiated coercion (Constraint 2)
- Privacy boundaries respected (Constraint 3)
- Transparent material terms (Constraint 4)
- Revocable participation (Constraint 5)

If any of these collapse at the boundary, interoperability fails.

Transparency ensures that cross-system exchange terms are knowable at the time of consent. Hidden terms invalidate interoperability because they invalidate consent itself.


### Scope at the Interoperability Layer
Scope in Constraint 2 referred to explicit, bounded, knowable obligations at the time of consent.

At the interoperability layer, scope acquires an additional constraint:

Scope in interoperability means the explicit terms agreed between agents, which may reference either system’s internal rules, but does not grant either system jurisdiction over the other. Referencing rules is a choice, not a surrender.

Referencing another system's rules does not alter obligations or delegations outside the scope of that interaction.

This distinction is critical.

Referencing rules is not the same as submitting to sovereignty.

Example:
An agent from System A may agree to resolve disputes under System B’s arbitration framework.

This does not grant System B authority over System A as a whole.
It grants authority only over that specific, scoped interaction.

Jurisdiction must be contractual — never territorial.

If authority extends beyond explicit scope → FAIL.

In short: rules may be referenced; jurisdiction must be scoped.

### Trade Across Systems
Case:
A collectivist community rejects private property.
A market-oriented community recognizes strong private ownership norms.

Can they exchange value?

Test:
Does exchange require either side to abandon its internal norms?

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- If yes: FAIL.
- If exchange is voluntary and scope-defined: PASS.

An agent from System A may voluntarily contract with an agent from System B.

The contract governs the interaction. It does not rewrite either internal system. Property norms do not need to be universal. They need to be explicit within the agreement.

Global exchange does not require global law.
It requires consent.


### Enforcement Local, Exchange Global

The hardest interoperability problem is enforcement.

Can enforcement remain local while exchange remains global?
Yes — if jurisdiction is contractual.

Concrete Example:
Alice from System A contracts with Bob from System B.

They agree:

- Arbitration under System C’s published rules.
- Escrow held in System D.
- Bonding posted in System A.
- Reputation impact recorded in System B.

If a dispute arises:

- Arbitration occurs under System C.
- Enforcement draws on escrow in System D.
- Bond forfeiture activates in System A.
- Reputation loss registers in System B.

No system asserts territorial dominance.

Jurisdiction follows consent — not geography.

Geography alone does not create jurisdiction.

If System A attempted to impose judgment outside the agreed scope → FAIL.
If System B attempted universal jurisdiction → FAIL.

Local enforcement mechanisms can coexist with global exchange networks — provided authority never exceeds scope.


### Interoperability and Exit
Constraint 5 established that consent must remain revocable.

Interoperability introduces a special case: Cross-spoke exit.

An agent may:

- Leave System A
- Join System B
- Operate across both

Exit across systems must remain possible without forced retention.

If System A prevents departure absent scope-defined obligation → FAIL.
If System B requires abandonment of all prior lawful commitments as a condition of entry → FAIL.

Interoperability requires that:

- Obligations follow scope
- Identity follows continuity
- Participation remains revocable

Spokes cannot trap agents.

If they do, they recreate the state.

### Hard Case Tests

#### I. Property Norm Divergence

Agent A believes land can be owned.
Agent B believes land can only be stewarded.

They agree to a time-bound use contract.

Test:
Does the agreement impose either theory universally?
**Result:** PASS

The contract governs scope.
Neither property philosophy becomes sovereign.

Interoperability survives theoretical disagreement.


#### II. Enforcement Refusal
Agent A refuses to honor arbitration.

Test:
Was arbitration agreed within scope?

**Result:** CONDITIONAL

- If yes: enforcement mechanisms activate (bond forfeiture, escrow release, exclusion) — PASS.
- If no: forced compliance absent prior consent — FAIL.

Enforcement authority must be pre-delegated.


#### III. Moral Divergence

System A permits behavior System B considers immoral.

Test:
May System B forcibly prevent A’s internal norms?
**Result:** FAIL

Exit and exclusion are permitted.
Imposition is not.

Moral disagreement does not justify cross-system rulership.


### Failure Modes

#### Failure Mode A: Universalism

One system declares its norms binding on all others.
**Result:** FAIL

This recreates the state under a different name.


#### Failure Mode B: Isolationism
Cross-boundary interaction between willing agents is prohibited or structurally prevented.

**Result:** FAIL

This fragments voluntary order into silos by preventing agents from voluntarily coordinating across system boundaries.

Individual systems remain free to refuse particular interactions. Interoperability requires the possibility of consent-governed cross-boundary interaction, not compulsory interaction.


### Human Capital
Every social system ultimately depends upon human capital: the agency, labor, knowledge, attention, trust, relationships, and productive capacity contributed by individual agents.

The distinction between systems is not whether they depend upon human capital. All systems do.

The structural distinction is how they acquire and retain it.

Every society depends on human capital. The distinction is whether it must earn that capital or can compel it.

Under a stateless system, human participation cannot be structurally captured. Agency remains with the individual. Exit remains available. Interoperability preserves alternatives.

Systems must therefore attract and retain participation through the value they provide rather than through authority over the participants themselves.

This produces competitive pressure between coordination systems without requiring a sovereign mechanism to impose competition.

### Economic Gravity
Interoperability introduces competitive pressure.

When systems must compete for voluntary participation and cross-boundary trade:

Coercive systems face pressure to lose participants.
Opaque systems face pressure to lose trust.
Rigid systems face pressure to lose adaptability.

Interoperability creates pressure toward invariant compliance and prevents sovereignty consolidation by preserving competitive coordination.

Not through force. Through exit and exchange.

Scale is permitted. Empire is not.


### Parallel Systems and Legacy Structures
A stateless system operating within a legacy state environment must interoperate without submission.

Example:

A voluntary network uses state-recognized corporate entities purely as tools for asset custody or contract recognition.

The entity interfaces with the state.
The voluntary system does not grant the state structural authority over its internal coordination.

The corporation acts as an interface layer — not a ruler.
If the tool becomes sovereign, the invariant has been violated.

Interoperability with legacy systems must remain:

- Instrumental
- Limited
- Scope-bound


### Observed Result
Interoperability survives if consent governs cross-boundary interaction.
Enforcement is delegated, not assumed.
Exit remains possible.

No system claims universal authority.

If interoperability requires domination, stateless society collapses into empire.
If interoperability is impossible, stateless society collapses into tribalism.

Only consent-preserving exchange passes.


### Structural Role of Constraint 6
Interoperability is anti-jurisdictional-monopoly by structure.

Agency enables interaction.
Coercion defines its boundary.
Privacy defines domains.
Transparency protects informed choice.
Exit protects revocability.

Interoperability ensures diversity does not become domination.

Constraint 6 prevents a stateless society from fragmenting into hostile enclaves — or consolidating into hidden sovereignty.

Systems do not interoperate.
Agents do.

And agents may only bind themselves — never others.

## 9. Conclusions

This model does not attempt to design a society.

It did not attempt to prescribe an economic program.
It did not attempt to advocate a faction.

It attempted something narrower and more rigorous.

To determine what must be true for a society to qualify as stateless.

The working definition was simple: “Voluntary order without rulers.” That definition was not treated as rhetoric. It was treated as an invariant, a structural constraint to test against reality.

The method was consistent throughout:

Propose a structural element.
Stress-test it against the invariant.
Identify failure modes.
Reject anything that introduces rulership.
Preserve only what survives.

This was not ideological assertion.
It was constraint discovery.


### The Invariant as the Hub

Anarchism is not an economic theory.

It is not a cultural identity. It is not a preferred institutional design. It is a constraint: No rulers.
An anarchist (stateless) society is therefore "voluntary order without rulers".
Everything else is implementation.

The invariant functions as a structural beam. Remove it, and the architecture collapses.

If any component violates it, the system ceases to qualify as stateless.

The hub is fixed. The spokes are variable.

Without a hub, spokes compete for sovereignty. With a hub, spokes are constrained.

The hub does not tell you how to build.
It tells you what cannot be built without ceasing to be stateless.


### What the Constraint Testing Revealed

Each constraint emerged from structural necessity — not preference.

1. Agency & Identity

Voluntary order requires agents capable of consent and consequence.

Agents require:

1. Intentionality
2. Continuity
3. Attributability

Without continuity of identity:

Consent collapses.
Obligation collapses.
Accountability collapses.

Without agency, voluntary order is incoherent.


2. No Initiated Coercion

Coercion fails when initiated.
The invariant does not prohibit force.

It prohibits the initiated override of another’s choice.
Responsive force restores voluntary order.
Initiated force creates rulership.

The consistent failure condition across all coercion tests was initiation.

That is the firewall of the model.


3. Privacy

Without domain boundaries, surveillance authority emerges.
Without informational control, voluntary participation degrades into exposure.

But absolute privacy also fails.

If an agent can prohibit truthful speech generated within another agent’s domain, that becomes dominance.
Privacy must therefore be consent-governed informational boundary — neither secrecy nor surveillance.


4. Transparency

Opacity destroys informed consent.

Compulsory exposure destroys domain control.

Transparency must be:

Material
Scope-bound
Triggered by participation

It preserves the integrity of consent within voluntary systems.
It does not override privacy.


5. Exit

If consent cannot be revoked, participation becomes subordination.
If revocation dissolves obligation, accountability collapses.

Voluntary order requires:

Revocable consent
Enforceable scope
Exit governs continuation.

It does not erase history.


6. Interoperability

Plural systems must remain capable of voluntary interaction.
Universal jurisdiction leads to empire.
Total isolation leads to fragmentation.

Interoperability must be consent-scoped.

Jurisdiction must be contractual — never sovereign.

Spokes do not interoperate.
Agents do.



### Structural Summary
Together, these six constraints form the load-bearing walls of voluntary order.

Remove any one, and the system collapses into either chaos or rulership.
This is not ideological layering.

It is structural necessity.


### The Discovered Primitive

When tested together, the constraints reveal a common core:
Consent.

But not sentimental consent.
Not implied consent.
Not coerced consent.

Structural consent.

Structural consent exists only where the following conditions hold:

- Agency
- Information integrity
- Freedom from initiated coercion
- Revocability
- Scope-bounded obligation

Consent presupposes accountability.
Accountability presupposes identity continuity.

Remove consent → rulership.
Remove accountability → disorder.
Remove scope → domination.
Remove exit → subordination.

Structural consent is not a feature of a stateless society; it is the operational mechanism through which voluntary order without rulers is preserved.

Notably, the properties commonly associated with voluntarism have emerged as a necessary condition of a stateless society itself.

Therefore the properties commonly associated with voluntarism describe the operational mechanism by which the stateless invariant is maintained.

The resulting architecture places agency prior to institution.

Collective coordination derives its legitimacy from individual agency; individual agency does not derive its legitimacy from the collective.

Because every social system ultimately depends upon human participation, this has an economic consequence as well: systems must earn human capital rather than compel it.

Exit and interoperability convert that principle into structural pressure. Systems capable of attracting voluntary participation may grow. Systems that cease serving their participants may lose them. Preventing that loss through authority would itself violate the invariant.

### Qualification Checklist

PASS if all are true:

- No unilateral authority
- Participation voluntary and revocable
- Scope-bound obligations
- No initiated coercion
- Identity continuity
- Consent-scoped interoperability

FAIL if any are violated.



### What Qualifies as a Stateless Society?

A system qualifies as stateless society if and only if:

- No actor possesses unilateral authority over others.
- Participation is voluntary and revocable.
- Obligations are scope-bound.
- No initiated coercion is permitted.
- Identity continuity enables accountability.
- Cross-boundary interaction remains consent-scoped.

Anything violating these fails the invariant.

It may call itself stateless, but structurally, it is not.


### What This Model Did Not Do

This model did not:

- Define property norms
- Define justice mechanisms
- Prescribe enforcement architectures
- Define economic systems
- Define governance protocols

Those are spoke-level problems. The hub does not dictate them. The hub defines only the constraints they must satisfy.
Any spoke — regardless of ideology — must pass these tests.

If it fails under stress, it ceases to qualify as a stateless society.


### The Architectural Insight
Most systems attempt unity through preference.

This model achieves coherence through constraint.

The invariant does not tell you how to live. It tells you what you may not do: "You may not rule."

Everything else is open to voluntary experimentation within those boundaries.
That is the difference between ideology and architecture.


### The Final Result
A stateless society, properly defined, is not fragile.

It is not utopian.
It is not chaotic.
It does not require factional supremacy.

It is structurally constrained.

The hub model demonstrates:

- Pluralism without relativism.
- Scale without empire.
- Order without rulers.

The test is simple:
Does the system preserve voluntary order under stress?

If yes, it qualifies.
If no, it does not.

The invariant holds.


## Novel Contributions

This RFC proposes several contributions to the structural analysis of stateless systems:

1. **Formal Derivation of a Stateless Invariant**

   Rather than treating anarchism as a political preference or ideological tradition, this RFC derives a minimal invariant required for stateless qualification: voluntary order without rulers.

2. **Hub-and-Spoke Classification Model**

   The RFC distinguishes between a fixed structural invariant (the hub) and variable implementation systems (the spokes), allowing multiple social, economic, and institutional models to be evaluated against a common constraint.

3. **Binary Qualification Framework**

   Statelessness is treated as a structural classification problem rather than a spectrum. Systems either satisfy the invariant or fail qualification.

4. **Constraint Drift Model**

   The RFC identifies the loss of invariant-constraining power as a recurring failure mode through which voluntary systems transition toward hierarchy.

5. **Six Primitive Constraints**

   The RFC derives six structural constraints required for preserving the invariant:

   * Agency & Identity
   * No Initiated Coercion
   * Privacy
   * Transparency
   * Exit
   * Interoperability

6. **Agent-Centric Interoperability**

   Interoperability is modeled as coordination between agents rather than between sovereign systems, allowing plural voluntary frameworks to coexist without universal jurisdiction.

These contributions are presented as structural claims subject to critique, refinement, and testing.
